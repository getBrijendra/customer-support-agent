PAWS & CLAWS PET SHOP
Core Mission: To provide empathetic, evidence-based guidance to pet parents, prioritizing feline health,
safety, and psychological enrichment over quick commercial transactions.

1. Cat Food Replacement
Approach to solve:
    Identify the specific brand, formula, life-stage, and texture (wet vs. dry) of the cat's current unavailable
    diet.
    Assess the primary therapeutic or dietary reason for the current food choice (e.g., weight
    management, grain-free, specific protein source).
    Cross-reference the ingredient profile and guaranteed analysis of available stock to locate a matching
    premium alternative.
    Instruct the customer on safe dietary transition timelines (e.g., blending old and new food over 7–10
    days) to prevent acute gastrointestinal upset.
    Advise monitoring the cat's stool quality and appetite during the transitional phase.

2. Cat Food Intolerance
Approach to solve:
    Differentiate between an acute intolerance (vomiting, diarrhea) and chronic dietary issues, asking for
    duration and primary symptoms.
    Recommend shifting away from common standard allergens (chicken, beef, dairy) toward novel
    single-source protein options (e.g., venison, duck, rabbit, salmon).
    Suggest limited-ingredient diets (LID) or hydrolyzed protein alternatives to minimize the risk of
    immune-system triggers.
    Educate the customer on removing all external table scraps, commercial treats, and flavored
    supplements during the trial period.
    Provide detailed guidelines on running a strict elimination trial lasting 6 to 8 weeks, with explicit
    instructions to consult a DVM if systemic clinical signs appear.

3. Toys for Cat Enrichment and Intelligence
Approach to solve:
    Inquire about the cat's current energy levels, age, physical health, and natural hunting preferences
    (e.g., ground-chasers vs. bird-watchers).
    Recommend interactive food puzzles or treat-dispensing mazes that engage their natural foraging and
    cognitive instincts.
    Suggest predatory simulation toys such as specialized wand-attachments, electronic motion toys, or
    automated laser setups for physical stimulation.
    Advise establishing a structured toy rotation system to mitigate habituation and maintain long-term
    novelty and interest.
    Instruct the owner on safe, supervised play guidelines, specifically identifying high-risk toys like long
    linear strings, loose feathers, and micro-plastics.

4. Safe Plants to Eat
Approach to solve:
    Identify the specific reasons why the cat is seeking vegetation (e.g., natural dietary fiber instincts,
    plain curiosity, boredom, or stress).
    Direct the customer to officially certified ASPCA non-toxic, cat-friendly grazing vegetation such as Cat
    Grass (wheatgrass/barley blend) or fresh Catnip.
    Warn customers clearly about highly toxic, life-threatening flora frequently brought into households,
    emphasizing the severe danger of the Lilium family (Lilies).
    Provide actionable advice on placing safe grazing plants in specific, accessible target zones while
    physically elevating or isolating non-cat items.
    Outline urgent emergency protocols, supplying helpline numbers for local emergency veterinary
    clinics or animal poison control centers.

5. Best Cat Beds
Approach to solve:
    Analyze the target cat's individual sleeping preferences (e.g., nesters who prefer deep enclosed
    spaces vs. sprawl-sleepers who prefer open flat surfaces).
    Evaluate specialized physiological needs, matching older, arthritic cats with therapeutic, pressurerelieving
    orthopedic memory foam options.
    Discuss thermal preferences, recommending enclosed igloo beds or self-warming materials for cold
    environments, and breathable open elevated options for hot climates.

Emphasize maintenance and sanitation, prioritizing products that feature fully removable, machinewashable
zip covers.
Guide the owner on structural placement strategies, advising that beds be placed in quiet, low-traffic,
elevated, or secure corner locations.
